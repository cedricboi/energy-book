## 2. Outcomes not covered by any lesson

All of 5(a) to 5(e) are covered. Two partial gaps:

1. **5(a)(iv) mechanical waves.** L2 has no sound scene, so students never see energy carried by a mechanical wave in the transfers lesson. Proposal: replace the "hand rubbing a cold metal spoon" scene (which repeats "mechanically" from the catapult) with a struck drum or a phone speaker, where energy leaves by propagation of sound waves. The phone hook already plays a song, so the speaker fits.
2. **5(c) "state the principle".** Students must state it, not only apply it. L5's notes page should have students complete the exact statement from the teacher's notes: "energy cannot be created or destroyed. Energy can be transferred from one store to another. The total energy of an isolated system is constant."

## 4. Exact syllabus wording to use

**Energy stores** (5(a)): kinetic, potential (gravitational, chemical, elastic), nuclear, internal. The notes write these as "kinetic store", "gravitational potential store", "internal store". The book follows the notes.

**Transfer pathways** (5(a)(i) to (iv)):
- Mechanically (by a force acting over a distance)
- Electrically (by an electric current)
- By heating (due to a temperature difference)
- By propagation of waves (both electromagnetic and mechanical)

**Equations and symbols** (Topic 5 and the key quantities table, page 22):

| Equation | Syllabus form | Symbols and units (page 22) |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic energy | Ek = ½ mv² | m in kg, v in m/s (speed symbol u, v), E in J |
| Gravitational potential energy | Ep = mgh | g in m/s² or N/kg, h (length) in m |
| Work done | work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force | work done W or E in J, force F in N |
| Power | power = energy transfer / time taken | power P in W, energy E in J or kW h, time t in s (also h, min, ms) |
| Weight (recalled) | weight = mass × gravitational field strength | weight W in N |

Note that the syllabus allows **W** for both weight and work done. The book should write "work done" in words or use **W** only where weight does not appear on the same page.

Definitions students must give (asterisked on page 22): work done, power, weight, speed, force.

**Value of g.** The syllabus does not state a value. The teacher's notes use **g = 10 N/kg** throughout ("10 m/s² = 10 N/kg"). Keep 10 N/kg.

**Principle of conservation of energy** (wording from the teacher's notes, since the syllabus asks students to state it but does not word it): "The principle of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. Energy can be transferred from one store to another. The total energy of an isolated system is constant."
