Lesson 1. Energy stores

Syllabus: 5(a)

Today's questions

  • Where is energy kept?

Page 1. A phone at 100% hook

Your phone is at 100% when you leave home in the morning. By night it is dead.

The energy has not vanished. So where was it kept, and where did it go? We start with where it was kept.

Page 3. Energy stores name

We describe where energy is kept as an energy store.

  1. The rolling toy car has energy in its kinetic store, because it is moving.
  2. The book held up high has energy in its gravitational potential store, because of its height.
  3. The stretched catapult has energy in its elastic potential store, because it is stretched.

  1. The battery has energy in its chemical potential store. Fuels and food do too.
  2. The hot kopi has energy in its internal store. The hotter it is, the more energy is in this store.
  3. The Sun has energy in its nuclear store, kept in the nuclei of its atoms.

Page 3. Four ways energy moves name

Energy moving from one store to another is an energy transfer. There are four ways.

  1. Transferred mechanically, by a force acting over a distance. The catapult band pushes the eraser.
  2. Transferred electrically, by an electric current. The current carries energy from the mains to the kettle.
  3. Transferred by heating, because of a temperature difference. The hot element heats the cooler water.
  4. Transferred by propagation of waves. The torch gives out light, an electromagnetic wave. The speaker gives out sound, a mechanical wave.

Page 4. Which way? use

l2-q1 A girl kicks a football. How is energy transferred from her foot to the ball?

  • Right. It is transferred mechanically. Right. Her foot pushes the ball with a force over a short distance.
  • It is transferred electrically. There is no electric current between a foot and a ball.
  • It is transferred by heating. Heating needs a temperature difference. The kick is a push.
  • It is transferred by propagation of waves. The thud of the kick is a sound wave, but the ball moves because of the push.

Page 3. Kinetic energy name

  1. Doubling the mass doubled the energy in the kinetic store.
  2. Doubling the speed made it four times as large. The energy depends on the speed squared.
  3. Kinetic energy, Ek = ½mv², where m is the mass in kg and v is the speed in m/s. Ek is in joules (J).

Page 4. The principle of conservation of energy name

  1. As she rolls down, her gravitational potential store falls and her kinetic store grows.
  2. As she rises, her kinetic store falls and her gravitational potential store grows again.
  3. The dark line on the ledger never moves. The total stays the same at every moment.
  4. This is the principle of conservation of energy. Energy cannot be created or destroyed. Energy can be transferred from one store to another. The total energy of an isolated system is constant.

Page 6. Where the energy goes name

  1. With friction, some energy is transferred mechanically to the internal store of the board, the wheels and the ramp on every pass.
  2. So less energy is left to lift her back up. Each pass ends lower.
  3. The dark line still does not move. The energy is not destroyed. It is spread out in the internal store of the surroundings, where it is hard to use again.

Glossary

energy store
A way of describing where energy is kept, such as the kinetic store of a moving car.
kinetic store
Energy an object has because it is moving.
gravitational potential store
Energy an object has because of its height above the ground.
elastic potential store
Energy an object has because it is stretched, squashed or bent.
chemical potential store
Energy kept in fuels, food and batteries, released by chemical reactions.
internal store
Energy of the particles in an object. A hotter object has more energy in its internal store.
nuclear store
Energy kept in the nuclei of atoms, released in nuclear reactions such as those in the Sun.
energy transfer
Energy moving from one store to another.
transferred mechanically
Energy transferred by a force acting over a distance.
transferred electrically
Energy transferred by an electric current.
transferred by heating
Energy transferred because of a temperature difference, from hotter to colder.
transferred by propagation of waves
Energy carried by waves, both electromagnetic (such as light) and mechanical (such as sound).
kinetic energy, Ek = ½mv²
The energy in the kinetic store, where m is the mass in kg and v is the speed in m/s.
gravitational potential energy, Ep = mgh
The energy in the gravitational potential store, where m is the mass in kg, g is the gravitational field strength and h is the height in m.
gravitational field strength, g
The gravitational force on each kilogram of mass. Near the Earth's surface, g = 10 N/kg.
principle of conservation of energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed. Energy can be transferred from one store to another. The total energy of an isolated system is constant.
internal store of the surroundings
Where energy goes when friction, air resistance or sound spread it out. It is not destroyed, but it is hard to use again.
work done
Work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force. Work done equals the energy transferred mechanically.
joule (J)
The unit of energy and of work done. 1 J is the work done when a force of 1 N moves an object 1 m. So 1 J = 1 N m.
power
Power = energy transferred ÷ time taken. It is how quickly energy is transferred.
watt (W)
The unit of power. 1 W is 1 J of energy transferred each second.
